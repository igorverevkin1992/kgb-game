## Title
Year of Spies (Russian: «Год шпионов»)

## Short description (max 300 characters)
Washington, 1985. You are a KGB counterintelligence officer at the Soviet embassy. Run
surveillance-detection routes, read motives, encrypt cables to Moscow and recruit sources —
while the FBI watches. A documentary noir played entirely at your desk.

## About the game

**Year of Spies** is a desk-bound espionage detective set in the most successful — and most
dangerous — years of Soviet intelligence in Washington. Autumn 1984: you arrive at the KGB
residency's counterintelligence line. A desk, a city map, a reel-to-reel recorder, a cipher
machine and a ledger. Two years to reach a source inside the CIA.

No gunfights. Documents, people and consequences.

- **Surveillance detection routes.** Three stops on the map and a notebook of sightings: the
  same pale-blue sedan at the pharmacy and at the dry cleaner's. Coincidence, or the FBI?
- **The motive board.** Money, ideology, compromise, ego. Find clues in reports, intercepts and
  press clippings, pin them up and build a case — three independent sources or nothing.
- **The "Fialka" cipher post.** Key from the one-time pad, the tape, the check group — and FBI
  forgeries among Moscow's directives. Every night you report to Moscow: the truth, an
  embellished version, or silence. Moscow checks.
- **Conversations on tape.** Empathy, business, pressure, flattery, and leverage from the
  dossier that only works at the right moment. Coercion recruits agents, not loyal ones.
- **Money leaves a trail.** Cash and Moscow escrow, currency transaction reports over $10,000,
  the new anti-money-laundering law — and an agent who starts living beyond his means.
- **The List.** Eighteen CIA assets in Moscow, the Centre's quotas and an arrest window — every
  arrest narrows Langley's mole hunt around your source. Then he has to pass the polygraph.

- 7–10 hour campaign in five operational phases, 1984–1986
- Six recruitment candidates, four endings, dozens of story lines
- No hidden numbers: risk thresholds and consequences are always visible
- "Archive 1985": real historical context and a documentary epilogue
- Forgiving saves (autosave, checkpoints, slots) and an optional one-save "Iron Curtain" mode

Characters and the operation are fictional; the historical background, laws and tradecraft are
based on open sources.
